Why we're different

Free is the easy part.

Plenty of tools are free until they aren't. Here's the stuff we can actually back up — no asterisks, no "trust us."

No price tag, no pivot

100% free. No ads, no “pro” wall.

The whole thing is free — every guest slot, every feature. We're not warming you up for a paid tier or stuffing ads between your guests. The public instance is free because P2P media is genuinely cheap to run: a little signaling and a tiny database, and that's about it.

Source-available · UEL v1.0

Read the code. Run the code. Keep it open.

GuestPass is licensed under the UEL v1.0 — not just "you can look," but a copyleft that keeps modified versions open too. Fork it, improve it, self-host it. What you can't do is quietly turn it into a closed paid clone.

P2P mesh

Your media goes browser-to-browser. We can't watch it.

Camera and mic travel directly between participants, end-to-end encrypted with DTLS-SRTP — our server never decodes a frame. If a guest's network forces a relay, you can bring your own TURN; it forwards encrypted packets it can't read either. We're honest about the limit: connection metadata (who's in a room, when) exists, because routing needs it.

Absolute-minimum data

The least we can keep and still work.

Your Google identity to sign you in, a name and email per guest so passes can be sent, and ephemeral session metadata while a room is live. No media, no chat logs, no third-party trackers. Guest names and emails are deleted within 24 hours of a stream ending. It's not "zero data" — that'd be a lie — it's the minimum the thing physically needs.

Everything the paid tools gate, none of the lock-in.

Built on plain WebRTC with a paper-thin backend that only relays who's who and a bit of signaling. Your camera and mic flow browser-to-browser — the server is never in the media path.

Zero-signup guests

Guests join from a browser tab with a magic link. No account, no install, works on a borrowed laptop.

Individual OBS sources

Every guest is their own clean browser source — composite them however you like in OBS, no baked-in grid.

Permissioned screen share

Flip a switch to let a guest share their screen. Off by default, revocable mid-stream.

P2P, with a fallback

Direct peer connections by default. Bring your own TURN relay for tricky networks; if a guest's network blocks peer-to-peer outright, they get a clear heads-up — never a silent hang.

You own the dials

Cap resolution, fps and bandwidth per stream so a flaky guest can't tank the whole broadcast.

Actually source-available

UEL v1.0. Run the free public instance at guest-pass.link, or self-host the same binary in one command. Read every line — and any hosted fork has to share its source too.
